The Stepford Wives

Ira Levin

pages —

1972
Joanna moves her family to a Connecticut commuter town and finds the wives there uncannily devoted to housework and to their husbands, with no interests of their own and no irritation about anything. Her one friend starts changing too. Short, cold, and the phrase it gave the language has outlived most of its imitators.

The Long Walk

Richard Bachman / Stephen King

370 pages

1979
One hundred teenage boys start walking south from the Maine border. Drop below four miles an hour and you get a warning; collect three and the soldiers on the halftrack shoot you. No finish line, only a last boy standing. King wrote it at eighteen and it is still the meanest thing he has published.

Perfume: The Story of a Murderer

Patrick Süskind

pages —

1985
Grenouille is born in the stink of a Paris fish market with no personal scent of his own and the finest nose in the world. Apprenticed to a perfumer, he sets out to capture the one smell he cannot synthesise, which belongs to young women and cannot be taken while they live.

Swan Song

Robert R. McCammon

956 pages

1987
Nuclear war, then the long walk through the ashes. A girl with an inexplicable gift, a wrestler turned protector, a drifter, and a thing wearing many faces that moves among the survivors encouraging the worst in them. A doorstopper apocalypse from the same era as The Stand and unembarrassed about it.

American Psycho

Bret Easton Ellis

399 pages

1991
Patrick Bateman, 26, Pierce & Pierce, reviews his skincare regimen and his business cards in the same flat voice he uses for the murders, and nobody in his circle can reliably tell him apart from the other interchangeable men in Valentino. Whether any of it happens is left open.

The Cipher

Kathe Koja

pages —

1991
Nicholas finds a hole in the storage room floor of his apartment building that is absolutely black and does things to whatever is lowered into it. He and Nakota cannot leave it alone. Early nineties transgressive horror about self-destruction as a vocation, and a landmark of the Dell Abyss line.

Exquisite Corpse

Poppy Z. Brite

pages —

1996
Two serial killers, an English aristocrat and a New Orleans predator, find each other and collaborate. Explicit about sex, murder and cannibalism in equal measure, set against the early AIDS years, and still the book publishers cite when they talk about what they would not print now.

Come Closer

Sara Gran

pages —

2003
Amanda is an architect with a good marriage and a small tapping sound in the apartment. Then the notes she does not remember writing, the cigarettes she does not smoke, the bruises on her husband. A hundred and sixty pages of possession narrated by a woman losing the argument for her own mind.

Haunted

Chuck Palahniuk

411 pages

2005
Seventeen people answer an ad for a writers' retreat, get locked in a derelict theatre, and set about sabotaging the food and each other so their eventual survival story will sell better. Framed around the stories each tells, including the notorious one about the swimming pool.

The Ruins

Scott Smith

pages —

2006
Four tourists in Mexico follow a German they met at the beach to a Mayan ruin off the map, and the Mayans at the treeline will not let them come back down. What is on the hill is a plant. Relentless, physiological, and almost entirely without hope.

John Dies at the End

David Wong

pages —

2007
A drug called soy sauce moves through a nowhere town and gives its users a view of things that should not be visible, including what is coming through. David and John are absolutely the wrong people to handle it. Comic horror that keeps getting genuinely frightening between jokes.

Survivor

J.F. Gonzalez

pages —

2007
A pregnant woman is separated from her husband on the highway and taken by people who make films of what they do to their subjects. Extreme horror from the Leisure paperback era, structured as an escape story, and notorious for how little it looks away.

The Passage

Justin Cronin

784 pages

2010
A military virus trial produces twelve immortal carriers, they get out, and America falls in about a hundred pages. Then a jump of a century to a walled colony in California running out of light, and a girl who does not age. Vampire apocalypse written with total literary seriousness.

A Short Stay in Hell

Steven L. Peck

pages —

2012
A devout geologist dies and learns the correct religion was Zoroastrianism. His hell is a Borgesian library of every possible book, which he must search for the volume telling his own life story before he can leave. A hundred pages, and the mathematics of it are the horror.

No One Gets Out Alive

Adam L.G. Nevill

pages —

2014
Stephanie takes a cheap room in a Birmingham house because she has no money and no options, and the landlord's brother is worse than the sounds at night. Nevill spends the first half on poverty and coercion as their own kind of haunting, then lets the house be what it is.

Revival

Stephen King

pages —

2014
A New England minister loses his family in an accident, denounces God from the pulpit, and returns to Jamie's life decades later as a travelling faith healer running on something he calls secret electricity. King has said it is the most frightening ending he has written, and the last twenty pages are Lovecraft rather than King.

A Head Full of Ghosts

Paul Tremblay

pages —

2015
Fourteen-year-old Marjorie is either schizophrenic or possessed, her father invites a priest and a reality television crew to settle it, and her younger sister Merry narrates the aftermath years later while a blogger picks the resulting show apart episode by episode. Tremblay never rules either reading out.

The Library at Mount Char

Scott Hawkins

pages —

2015
Carolyn and her adopted siblings were raised by a man they call Father in a library of twelve catalogues, one per discipline: languages, war, healing, death. Father is missing and the succession is open. Absolutely unpredictable, extremely violent, and funnier than that suggests.

Fantasticland

Mike Bockoven

272 pages

2016
A hurricane cuts off a Florida theme park with its staff inside, and five weeks later they are extracted having split into warring tribes by costume department. Told as an oral history of interviews after the fact, which keeps the reader assembling it.

I'm Thinking of Ending Things

Iain Reid

183 pages

2016
A woman drives with her new boyfriend to his parents' remote farm, planning to break up with him, and the visit goes wrong in ways that keep sliding sideways. Short, claustrophobic, and the ending recontextualises every page.

The Black Farm

Elias Witherow

pages —

2016
A man kills himself to escape his marriage and arrives somewhere worse: a farm where the dead are processed by things that are not remotely human, and where suicide is the entry requirement. Grew out of internet horror and keeps that relentless, image-driven brutality.

The Fisherman

John Langan

pages —

2016
Two widowers from the same company go fishing together because it is the only thing that helps, and a stranger tells them about Dutchman's Creek. The long middle section is a folk history of a drowned Catskills valley and what was bargained with there. Grief-driven cosmic horror, elegiac rather than gory.

The Cabin at the End of the World

Paul Tremblay

272 pages

2018
Two dads and their daughter are at a lake cabin when four strangers arrive and explain, apologetically, that the family must choose one of themselves to kill or the world ends. Tremblay never confirms whether they are right, and the book is unbearable for it.

Woom

Duncan Ralston

pages —

2018
Angel checks into room 6 of a filthy motel, pays a sex worker to listen, and tells her stories about the building and what has happened in it, each one worse than the last, working toward why he chose this room. Body horror and birth horror, and among the more infamous of the extreme small-press titles.

Slewfoot: A Tale of Bewitchery

Brom

pages —

2021
Connecticut, 1666. Abitha is newly widowed and about to be taken apart by her husband's brother and the village, while something ancient wakes in the woods that cannot remember whether it is a protector or a slaughterer. Brom illustrates his own books and the folk-horror imagery is the point.

Tell Me I'm Worthless

Alison Rumfitt

264 pages

2021
Three years ago Alice and Ila went into a derelict house and came out with incompatible accounts of what happened. The house is fascism, literally and not metaphorically, and it wants them back. Furious, formally aggressive, and explicit about transphobia as a haunting.

Just Like Home

Sarah Gailey

pages —

2022
Vera returns to the house her father built, where he also killed at least six people, because her dying mother summoned her and there is an artist in residence in the guest house making work out of the murders. The house is not neutral about her being back.

The Reformatory

Tananarive Due

pages —

2023
Florida, 1950. Twelve-year-old Robbie Stephens is sentenced to six months at the Gracetown School for Boys for kicking a white boy, and can see the haints of the children who died there and were never accounted for. Built on the real Dozier School, and it won the Bram Stoker.

Incidents Around the House

Josh Malerman

pages —

2024
Eight-year-old Bela narrates, and the thing she calls Other Mommy lives in the closet and keeps asking a single question: can it go inside her heart. The child's voice is the entire engine, and it stays a child's voice while the house comes apart.

Vampires at Sea

We Used to Live Here

Marcus Kliewer

312 pages

2024
Eve and her girlfriend Charlie flip houses, and they have just bought a big old place in the woods. Then a man knocks: he grew up here, his family is in the car, could they look around, just for a minute. Eve says yes. They do not leave.

We Love You, Bunny

Mona Awad

496 pages

2025
The sequel to Bunny, and this time the Bunnies get the microphone. They have a great deal to say about Samantha, about what really went on in that workshop, and about who was using whom. Cotton-candy menace at nearly five hundred pages.

When the Wolf Comes Home

Nat Cassidy

304 pages

2025
A struggling actress finds a five-year-old runaway in the bushes outside her apartment, and a bloody encounter with the boy's father sends the two of them running from something much worse than a man. Presents as a werewolf novel, is really about inherited fear. Stephen King called it a classic.
